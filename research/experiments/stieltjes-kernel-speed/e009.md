## Interpretation
The total, measured the way backlog item 3 asks: against the frozen baseline in the same job on the same node, the final
kernel takes the `wstieltjes_q3_d2_xs` step from 13.64 to 6.08 s on the trained model (-55.4 %) and from 13.61 to 6.92 s
from scratch (-49.1 %), with the same loss at every step and, from the e008 gate, bit-identical outputs and gradients.
Both pre-profile predictions are met (>= 20 % lower with the gate passed, held at 55 %; reaching ASEntmax's 10.0 s, held
at 30 %): the sparse Stieltjes rows are now 30 % (from scratch) to 39 % (trained) below ASEntmax per step, and the
trained step is within a few percent of softmax_xs (3.3 % below it with softmax at its better split, 16 x 16).
What the new rows add:
- The baseline has no value-dependent work: trained and from-scratch steps agree to 0.2 %, as do ASEntmax's (0.1 %).
  Only the final Stieltjes kernel gets faster as the heads sparsify (6.92 -> 6.08 s, 12 %), so the cost of a real run is
  between the two rates and where it sits was not measured.
- The gain carries across the family: the AS row 0.456 of baseline (trained), q4/d2 0.511 from scratch (q3/d2 0.509),
  and a narrower window is faster still, q3/d1.5 trained 0.403 (5.49 s, 9.7 % below the q3/d2 trained step; the two
  baselines differ by 0.14 % across the two nodes, so the cross-node comparison holds).
- The AS row fits 40 GB at mb 16 x 16 and gains 4.9 %, but with 38.3 GB reserved it has under 2 GB of headroom on a
  40 GB A100.
Limits: one checkpoint per row (lr 1.2e-3, seed 1337), 125M / T=2048 only; the speed depends on how sparse the trained
heads are, so 350M and other (q, d) need their own timing; softmax_xs was timed from scratch only and at 16 x 16 while
the sparse rows ran 8 x 32 (same-split numbers are in e008: trained Stieltjes 7.2-7.7 % below softmax_xs); 27 steps per
run, sd 0.02 s, node-to-node spread 2 %.
