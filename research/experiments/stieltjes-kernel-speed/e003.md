## Hypothesis
After e002 the forward kernel is still 43-46 % of the 8.91 s step (10.0 ms x 384 calls) and the backward 15 %. Two
candidates could cut that, and which one is worth building is a measurement, not a guess:
(a) launch parameters. The 64x32 tiles and the Triton defaults (4 warps, 3 stages) were chosen on the unrolled kernel
(asentmax job 7462343); on the runtime-loop kernel another tile shape, `num_warps` or `num_stages` may be faster.
(b) tile skipping. The Newton iteration starts left of the root and lambda rises, so the support only shrinks: a
(64-row block, 32-key tile) tile with no key in any row's support at sweep t contributes exact zeros to every later
sweep, to the output sweep and to the backward. Trained heads keep ~20-40 keys per row of 2048, so many tiles may be
dead; but a block is 64 rows and its tiles are only dead if dead for all of them, so the union over rows may leave
most tiles live at N=2048 (32 causal tiles per block on average). The NAPE heads with large slopes are local and
should be nearly all dead away from the diagonal.
No kernel edit in this experiment; it produces the table the next candidates are chosen from.

  64x32 remains the fastest tile shape at the default options: 50 %.

## Change
- `bench_kernel.py sweep`: launches the candidate's `_fwd_kernel` and the three backward kernels directly with other
  BLOCK_M / BLOCK_N and Triton launch options (`num_warps`, `num_stages` are read from the launch kwargs by
  `CUDABackend.parse_options` in triton 3.1.0, checked in the installed source). Forward: tiles {32, 64, 128} x
  {16, 32, 64, 128} at the defaults, then warps {2, 4, 8} x stages {1, 2, 3, 4} on the three fastest tile shapes;
  backward kernels: tiles {32, 64, 128} x {32, 64} x warps {4, 8}, then stages {1, 2, 4} on the fastest; the default
  and the best variants re-timed back to back (5 repeats) and the forward ones at N=16384. Every variant is compared
  bit for bit with the default launch per output. Variants that do not compile or exceed shared memory are recorded.
- `bench_kernel.py tiles`: fp32 emulation of the kernel's Newton loop on captured q/k (same capture as the gate), per
  layer: live-tile fraction at each sweep's lambda and at the final lambda for tiles 64x32, 32x32, 32x16, 64x64; the
  fraction a row-max sweep could already exclude; keys per row at lambda_0 and at the end; sweeps until lambda stops
  changing per 64-row block; rows where lambda decreases; tiles that come back.
- `osc/kernel_measure.sbatch` (`ks_meas`, debug-nextgen, 1 GPU, 1 h limit): tiles, then sweep; json written after
  every row so a walltime kill keeps what was measured.
- No change to `stieltjes/triton_stieltjes.py` or `model.py`.
