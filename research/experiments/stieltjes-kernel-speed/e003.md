## Hypothesis
After e002 the forward kernel is still 43-46 % of the 8.91 s step (10.0 ms x 384 calls) and the backward 15 %. Two
candidates could cut that, and which one is worth building is a measurement, not a guess:
(a) launch parameters. The 64x32 tiles and the Triton defaults (4 warps, 3 stages) were chosen on the unrolled kernel
(asentmax job 7462343); on the runtime-loop kernel another tile shape, `num_warps` or `num_stages` may be faster.
(b) tile skipping. The Newton iteration starts left of the root and lambda rises, so the support only shrinks: a
(64-row block, 32-key tile) tile with no key in any row's support at sweep t contributes exact zeros to every later
sweep, to the output sweep and to the backward. Trained heads keep ~20-40 keys per row of 2048, so many tiles may be
dead; but a block is 64 rows and its tiles are only dead if dead for all of them, so the union over rows may leave
most tiles live at N=2048 (32 causal tiles per block on average). The NAPE heads with large slopes are local and
should be nearly all dead away from the diagonal.
No kernel edit in this experiment; it produces the table the next candidates are chosen from.

## Interpretation
Launch parameters are exhausted: the tiles and options chosen on the unrolled kernel are still the best on the
runtime-loop kernel, and the one backward gain on offer (0.15 ms of a 13.6 ms forward + backward, about 1 %) would give
up bit identity. That candidate is closed without a kernel edit. Early exit is closed too: under bit identity a block
cannot stop before sweep 15 because lambda is still moving in 86 % of them.
Tile skipping is the candidate with headroom. On trained inputs 53 % of the tiles the forward sweeps are already
outside every row's support at the first Newton sweep and 65 % at the end, so a mask carried from sweep to sweep would
do 7.5 of 17 sweeps of tile work at N=2048 (6.8 at N=16384), and the three backward kernels would do 35 % (29 %) of
theirs. If a skipped tile costs nothing that is the forward at ~4.9 ms instead of 10.1 and the step at ~6.6 s instead
of 8.9; a skipped tile will not cost nothing (a test per tile, and the K-tile loads inside a branch are probably no
longer pipelined, which e003 prices at +8 % per processed tile from the `num_stages=1` row).
Two limits on what this table shows. (1) It is the end-of-training state of two checkpoints: at initialisation the
logits are flat, every key is inside the window and no tile is dead, so the saving over a whole training run is smaller
than the end-state number and has to be measured on a trained model, not on a from-scratch timing. (2) "No tile comes
back" is an observation on 48 cases, not a property of the solver, since lambda does decrease by rounding; a kernel
that relies on it would not be exact by construction.
