## Hypothesis
The 7.3 s/step that `wstieltjes_q3_d2_xs` costs over softmax at 125M / T=2048 is mostly kernel time, and within the
kernel mostly the forward (17 QK^T sweeps at num_iter 15 against 9 dot-sweeps in the three backward kernels), so the
Newton loop is where a speed-up has to come from. No kernel change in this experiment: it builds the correctness gate
and the profile every later candidate is chosen from.

## Prediction (before submission)
From the brief (to be scored as written):
- the measured 125M step of `wstieltjes_q3_d2_xs` (unmodified `train.py`, mb 8 x 32, compile on) is 13.7 +- 0.7 s: 80 %
- the forward kernel is > 50 % of the Stieltjes attention time: 60 %
- attention is > 60 % of the Stieltjes step: 65 %

Added by the agent before submission:
- forward kernel time is affine in NUM_ITER; per-sweep cost from (it=15 - it=0)/15 and from (it=30 - it=15)/15 agree
  within 10 %: 85 %
- by dot-sweep count (fwd 18, bwd 9) the forward is 60-72 % of fwd+bwd kernel time: 55 %
- non-kernel overhead at the call site (`.contiguous()` copies, the `torch.compiler.disable` boundary) is < 3 % of the
  attention time: 80 %
- micro-batch 16 x 16 for `wstieltjes_q3_d2_xs` OOMs on 40 GB (the reason `run_lm.sh` halves it): 60 %
- gate: candidate == baseline bit for bit (same source), so every ratio is 1.0 and the e2e loss difference is 0: 97 %.
  The informative part is the frozen baseline against eager: it is inside the `test_attn_paths.py` tolerances on every
  real-input case (trained supports of 20-40 keys, N=2048 and N=16384, num_iter 15): 75 %; max |S - 1| at num_iter 15
  on real inputs < 1e-4 (the `probe_decide.py` criterion): 80 %.
