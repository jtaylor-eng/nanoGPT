## Hypothesis
The 7.3 s/step that `wstieltjes_q3_d2_xs` costs over softmax at 125M / T=2048 is mostly kernel time, and within the
kernel mostly the forward (17 QK^T sweeps at num_iter 15 against 9 dot-sweeps in the three backward kernels), so the
Newton loop is where a speed-up has to come from. No kernel change in this experiment: it builds the correctness gate
and the profile every later candidate is chosen from.

## Interpretation
The brief's theory holds in outline (the step is dominated by the forward kernel: 9.5 of 13.6 s) but its counter-
argument does not: the sweeps are not bound by the QK^T dot. A sweep costs 0.6 ms when the kernel holds 7 of them and
2.0 ms when it holds 32, and the row-max plus output sweeps of the NUM_ITER 0 kernel cost under 1.5 ms together. The
cost that grows with NUM_ITER is in how the kernel is built, not in the arithmetic: `tl.static_range(NUM_ITER)` is
unrolled by Triton 3.1.0 (`code_generator.visit_For` visits the body once per iteration), so the num_iter 15 kernel
contains 17 copies of the tile loop. If a sweep cost 0.6 ms regardless of count, the forward would be ~10 ms instead
of 22.8 ms and the step ~8.7 s, below ASEntmax (9.88 s). Which resource the copies exhaust (registers, code size,
shared memory per block) is not identified by e001.
Not ruled out by e001: that the growth is something else that scales with NUM_ITER in the unrolled kernel only by
coincidence; e002 tests the loop form directly with everything else fixed.
Other entries of the table: the backward is 10 % of the step (3 kernels, 3.4 ms), so backward work can give at most
~1.4 s; everything that is not attention is 3.2 s, the floor for kernel work is therefore ~4.6 s/step. The call site
is not worth touching (0.1 s). The 1 ms `_pick_blocks` device query per forward call is CPU time while the GPU is
busy with a 10-25 ms kernel, so removing it is not expected to change the GPU-bound step; it is a clean-up, not a
candidate. Micro-batch 16 x 16 fits for the sparse row on these 4 steps but is only 1 % faster (13.45 against 13.58)
and leaves 1.9 GB of the 40 GB unreserved; `run_lm.sh` is outside this direction's files, so it is reported, not
changed. The long-context forward runs at the same rate per sweep as the training shape (173.7 ms for 8x the MACs),
so whatever fixes the training shape should carry to 16k eval.
