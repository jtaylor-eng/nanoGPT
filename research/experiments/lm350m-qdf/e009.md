## Interpretation
A length-dependent temperature with no sparsity gets about half of the sparse rows' long-document advantage at 125M, at
softmax cost and with no train-length penalty.
- Train length: ssmax ties softmax (3.402 vs 3.403) and therefore the whole e006 tie (3.394-3.399 means). Nothing separates
  any row at 2048.
- Long documents at lr 1.2e-3: ArXiv 16k 3.979 sits between softmax (4.215-4.283) and the sparse rows (3.604-3.741), well
  outside both ranges. The curve is flat past the training length, where softmax rises and the sparse rows keep falling.
  So the softmax collapse is partly a temperature effect (the hypothesis' first branch, about 45 %), and the remaining
  half needs what SSMax lacks: exact zeros or a per-query (content-dependent) scale. e010 tests an entropy-dependent
  per-query temperature without sparsity; e008's AS-windowed row and ASEntmax have both.
- FineWeb 16k is slightly worse than softmax (+0.027, outside softmax's 0.006 seed range), the same sign as every sparse
  row (Stieltjes 3.440-3.513, ASEntmax 3.415-3.645). The FineWeb 8k -> 16k uptick is therefore not specific to sparsity:
  sharpening the NoPE heads with length costs a little on in-distribution text and pays on long documents. Softmax remains
  the best row at FineWeb 16k.
- LR sensitivity: at 2.4e-3 the gain disappears (ArXiv 16k 4.225, above softmax 2.4e-3 at 4.144) although the scaled heads
  are sharper (p_max 0.20 vs 0.10). With e007 (Stieltjes q3/d2 2.4e-3: ArXiv 16k 3.652 -> 4.503) this is the second
  length-adaptive mechanism whose extrapolation breaks at 2.4e-3 while plain softmax improves there. Sharper is not
  better beyond the training length; what the higher LR changes is not visible in entropy or p_max alone. One seed per
  cell, and +0.081 vs softmax 2.4e-3 is only 1.2x the softmax seed range, so "no gain at 2.4e-3" is the supported claim,
  not "worse than softmax".
Caveats: one seed; SSMax here is on the 6 NoPE heads only (ALiBi heads plain softmax), s_h initialised at 1.
