## Interpretation
The LR optimum is bracketed for the two sparse rows and not for softmax. Stieltjes q3/d2 is best at 1.2e-3 (2.4e-3 is +0.018 at
train length, 4x its seed range); ASEntmax is best at 1.2e-3 with a stability ceiling between 1.2e-3 and 2.4e-3 (NaN at iter
1690 without a loss spike, the same failure as at 350M / 1.2e-3, so the ceiling moves down with size). Softmax prefers 2.4e-3
by 0.008 at val@2048, a margin equal to its own seed range, and by more than its seed range on ArXiv 16k (-0.125).
So the e005 / e006 three-way tie at val@2048 stands with softmax at 2.4e-3 too: best-LR numbers 3.395 / 3.405 / 3.396
(softmax / ASEntmax / Stieltjes, seed 1337), spread 0.010. The e006 softmax seeds were run at 1.2e-3, i.e. not at softmax's
selected LR; the difference (0.008) is small against the 0.5+ long-range gaps but equal to the train-length differences
being compared, so "Stieltjes <= softmax at val@2048" (e006, -0.005 on means) is not established against softmax at 2.4e-3.
New observation: the sparse map does not by itself give long-range generalization. Stieltjes q3/d2 at 2.4e-3 has a narrower
support than at 1.2e-3 and still loses 0.85 at ArXiv 16k, ending worse than softmax (4.503 vs 4.144-4.269), while its FineWeb
16k is fine. My reading, not verified: at the higher LR the heads settle into a sharper, more local solution that is adequate
on web text but does not use long-document context; the per-head `stats` in arxiv.json / lengths_16k.json would be where to
look. It is one run at one seed; the size of the effect (~39x the Stieltjes ArXiv 16k seed range) makes a seed fluke unlikely
but a rerun at another seed is the only way to rule it out.
