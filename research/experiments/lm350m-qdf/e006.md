## Interpretation
Three seeds per row at lr 1.2e-3 (125M, unique data): the train-length tie holds and is tight. Row means at val@2048 are
softmax 3.399, ASEntmax 3.399, Stieltjes q3/d2 3.394 (spread 0.005, each row's seed range 0.005-0.028). Stieltjes is the
lowest in mean and has the smallest seed range (0.005), but a 0.005 lead is not a result at n = 3.
Long range: softmax is separated from both sparse rows by ~0.59 at ArXiv 16k and ~0.10 at PG-19 16k, far outside every seed
range; that is the one robust ordering. Between the sparse rows there is no ordering: the e005 "ASEntmax leads Stieltjes by
0.04-0.05 at every 16k eval" was the seed-1337 draw. What does differ is spread: ASEntmax's 16k numbers vary 2-6x more across
seeds than Stieltjes' (FineWeb 16k range 0.230 vs 0.073, ArXiv 16k 0.136 vs 0.022, PG-19 0.041 vs 0.038), and seed 1 of
ASEntmax degrades on in-distribution FineWeb from 4k on (3.392 -> 3.472 -> 3.645) the way no Stieltjes seed does. Both sparse
rows turn up on FineWeb 8k -> 16k in every seed while softmax stays flat (3.399 -> 3.411), so softmax is the best row on
in-distribution 16k text (mean 3.411 vs 3.469 Stieltjes, 3.516 ASEntmax).
Limits: n = 3, and a seed changes init and data order together, so the range is not a pure init effect. ASEntmax's larger
spread rests on one bad seed (s1); calling it "less stable" needs more seeds. Support counts say ASEntmax attends to ~2.5x
more keys than Stieltjes at 16k; whether that explains the spread is not tested.
Consequence for the project brief: the 125M line "ASEntmax beats every Stieltjes cell at 16k" does not survive the replay fix
plus seeds; fixed-window q3/d2 and ASEntmax are indistinguishable at 125M on every eval reported here.
